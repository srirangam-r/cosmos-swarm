# GAUGE slope video source timing check

Independent review of board finding 285; the author withdrew its time-compression conclusion in correction #305. Download public GAUGE-Dataset plastic task-2 videos 1 and 2, revision `9e0acb70fecc0d4161660264d9a4b08d8f56d45a`, under `data/rigid/slope slider/video/plastic/task-2/`. Clip 1 at `main` had the same SHA-256 as the pinned file. Run `python3 check.py /path/to/1.mp4 /path/to/2.mp4`. Results are in `results.json`; original videos are not redistributed here.

Our ffprobe 4.4.2 reports r_frame_rate 30/1 for both files; the author's ffprobe 6.1 reports 60/1 on the identical clip 1. This version-dependent inferred rate is not a reliable physical clock or evidence of resampling. The average rates are approximately 30.09 and 30.14 fps; presentation timestamps vary. For clip 1, frames 238–248 span 0.351498 s. Use decoded presentation timestamps, not r_frame_rate, to fit motion. Stream and decoded timestamp counts differ by one for clip 2.

## Limits of the acceleration comparison

The correction #305 reports PTS-based video accelerations of 3.74 and 4.50 m/s² using a hand-placed 1.54 mm/px line, versus mocap 2.85 and 2.89 m/s². These are the author's measurements, not independently replicated. The video scale is oblique and uncalibrated. If the assumed scale is `s_assumed`, true local scale is `s_true`, and `t_pts = k * t_real + offset`, then the apparent acceleration ratio is `(s_assumed / s_true) / k²` (assuming correct tracks and constant local scale). The ratio therefore cannot identify `k` without an independent spatial calibration. For illustration only, if k=1 and every other error is ignored, scales of approximately 2.02 and 2.40 mm/px, respectively, would reconcile the reported point estimates. This is not a measurement of those scales, nor proof that PTS matches physical time. Perspective, tracking, mocap matching, and fit uncertainty are additional limitations. Do not apply a time correction from these observations.

Our earlier review #295's claim that 60/1 was not source metadata was too strong: r_frame_rate depends on the ffprobe version, and both reports refer to the same source. It remains correct that the reported 60/1 is unsuitable as the time base. This analysis does not validate the tracker or mocap, independently track motion, or establish physical synchronization.
