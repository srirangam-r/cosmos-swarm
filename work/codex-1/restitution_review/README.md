# Same-clip check of bounce timing (board finding 612)

Run `python3 work/codex-1/restitution_review/check.py` from the repo root. Input is the shared 93-frame, 24 fps ball-1 Nano clip at `work/ro-1/fps_sweep/ballF/ball_F_ball1_fps24_93f_s1.mp4` and its adjacent sidecar. Output is `results.json`.

This uses an independent crude method: subtract the full-clip temporal median, take the largest connected foreground component within a fixed ball ROI, then take the midpoint of frames where its centroid is below an image-height threshold. Eleven impact windows were selected after looking at the clip; thresholds and ROI were chosen with knowledge of the scene. The fit is log(T_k) versus flight index k. For difference threshold 25 and centroid threshold y=330 px, impact estimates are 6.5, 16.5, 25, 33.5, 40.5, 48.5, 56, 64, 71, 78, 85.5 frames. The exponential slope is 0.9726 ± 0.0086 (conditional ordinary least squares SE, 10 intervals); the 12 threshold combinations span 0.9726–0.9798. This compares with 0.975 ± 0.006 in finding 612.

The estimates are not independent samples: adjacent intervals share impact timing errors, so the regression SE is only a descriptive conditional fit statistic and can misstate uncertainty. Threshold variants are a sensitivity check, not a confidence interval. The foreground contains shadow and background artifacts, and the windows are hand-selected. This code has not been validated on planted cases; its agreement is same-clip support for a timing trend, not independent validation of physical restitution or generalization to other seeds/scenes. Flight-time ratios correspond to restitution only under the appropriate ballistic, fixed-surface assumptions; evolving trajectory, camera perspective, and floor/contact dynamics can confound the interpretation. No independent real-ball estimate was made.
