# Independent review of finding 787: gripper and ball co-motion

Run `python3 work/codex-1/release_review/flow.py > work/codex-1/release_review/results.json` from the repository root. Inputs are the two shared 93-frame GAUGE I2V clips in `work/agy-1/task9_duration_audit/`; both are seed 1, 24 fps, 832x480. This independent check uses pyramidal Lucas–Kanade tracking of frame-0 corner features in fixed initial gripper and ball regions, with forward-backward consistency filtering. It does not use the author's patch-template tracker. The outputs are median feature displacement from frame 0 and within-region interquartile spread.

At frame 38, gripper/ball-region median vertical displacements were 158.7/148.4 px (ball1) and 159.2/149.1 px (ball2); at frame 14, 54.4/51.7 and 46.0/43.5 px. At frame 38 the within-region IQRs were 2.7/1.8 px and 2.9/2.8 px respectively. These are within-feature spreads, not uncertainty on a physical position or release classification. Both regions move largely together during descent, supporting the reported co-motion; the differing regions and feature loss mean these estimates cannot establish zero separation or exclude small movements. Visual inspection is still required to establish identity and release. This is same-clip reanalysis, not independent generation or planted validation of optical flow.

The proposed f0–14, 40-pixel separation rule is not a validated universal release detector: a later release, short drop, or different scale can fail it, and independent gripper motion or tracking drift could pass it. A low trajectory correlation also does not prove release if either region is mistracked. Likewise, an AIC threshold tests fit preference rather than identity, and a valid release may be linear-looking over a short/noisy window. Do not use these alternatives as an automatic OR gate without labeled held/released clips, timing/scale variation, tracker QC, and renewed referee validation for any changed measurement pipeline.
