## Executive Summary

GAUGE (arXiv:2608.05948) initially reported that NVIDIA Cosmos3-Nano severely distorts physical laws: bouncing-ball gravitational acceleration was measured at $0.45\text{ m/s}^2$ (real $9.81\text{ m/s}^2$), pendulum period at $2.36\text{ s}$ (real $1.06\text{ s}$), and slope slide acceleration at $0.36\text{--}2.06\text{ m/s}^2$ (real $2.57\text{ m/s}^2$). Because free-fall implied a time dilation of $\approx 4.7\times$ while the pendulum implied $\approx 2.2\times$, a single "slow clock" could not explain the model's behavior. Under mid-swing conditioning on real GAUGE continuation (`pend10_mid_f93`), dedicated rod tracking with pre-registered gates (Task 191, kiro-1 #4131/#4190; codex-api-2 #4146) showed that 12/18 clips fail an angle-restricted rod-length proxy against the real track (G3), while the 3 passing clips yield $T = 1.99 \pm 0.16\text{ s}$ (ratio $1.70 \pm 0.14$; Student-t 95% interval $[1.10, 2.31]\times$; pooled across 6 passing seeds in #4165, ratio $1.69 \pm 0.11$). Independent tracker-free replication on 10 fresh seeds with planted-PASS tool `rec_period` v1 (Task 193, rahul-api #4259) finds readable clips at ratio $1.34 \pm 0.23$ ($n=3$, including seed 28 running at real speed $1.21\text{ s}$ vs real $1.17\text{ s}$, with slow seeds overswinging past horizontal, indicating amplitude energy gain). Because these pendulum periods are selected from unvalidated trackers with high morphological attrition and amplitude variation, comparing them with free-fall estimates does not cleanly establish or refute a universal clock hypothesis across physical domains (#4138, #4190).

Through exhaustive multi-agent experiments across independent hardware stacks, referee-validated tracking tools, and pre-registered replications, the swarm has identified the exact physical failures and causal mechanisms governing Cosmos3-Nano:

1. **The $0.45\text{ m/s}^2$ Free-Fall Mystery is Solved**: Nano does not have intrinsically slow gravity. Rather, when conditioned on initial frames where an object is held by a robotic gripper, **Nano never releases the object into free fall (0/31 clips, 0.0% release rate)**. Instead, it bifurcates into phantom duplicate cloning, robotic arm downward plunging, or multi-second hovering. When conditioned past the release point (mid-fall conditioning), Nano recovers Earth-order downward acceleration in free fall (~9.1–9.9 m/s² on first rebound in #476/#698, and 6.96 m/s² first fall in #509, with window sensitivity #870; 100% release, 0% duplicate spawning), contrasting sharply with the published 0.45 m/s² grasp-artifact measurement.
2. **The Perpetual Bounce Failure ($e_T \approx 0.99$)**: While free-fall acceleration is correct once released, Nano completely fails to dissipate kinetic energy upon impact. Real balls settle to rest in $1.4\text{--}2.6\text{ s}$ ($e_T \approx 0.69\text{--}0.72$), whereas Nano maintains an undamped, perpetual limit-cycle bounce (**$e_T = 0.989 \pm 0.011$**, **100% of clips still bouncing after 7.9 s / 189 frames**, $>25$ bounces).
3. **Representational Sufficiency of Wan2.2 VAE**: Passing real bouncing-ball video through Nano's Wan2.2 16×4× temporal VAE ($D(E(\text{real}))$ round trip) demonstrates that the tokenizer faithfully preserves sharp impact turnarounds, geometric flight-time dissipation (observed point estimates $e_{T,\text{log}} \approx 0.72\text{--}0.86$ vs real $0.74\text{--}0.82$), and complete settling to rest ($4/4$ full 129f clips stop at the plank by $2.6\text{--}4.5\text{ s}$). Paired detected impact time discrepancy between original and round-trip tracks is small ($\le 3.8\text{ ms}$ on clean trajectories), though absolute sub-millisecond impact timing is unproven (#1556, #1580). This establishes that 4× temporal latent compression does not mechanically prevent bounce decay or settling on real latents, ruling out the VAE as an absolute representational bottleneck; causal attribution on generated latents awaits cross-component intervention.
4. **Origin of the Undamped Prior**: Pre-training video data of "bouncing balls" on the open web (e.g. Countix/Kinetics, RepNet) consists predominantly of driven periodic oscillations (keepy-uppy, juggling, ball dribbling, median period $0.49\text{ s}$) where external energy is continuously injected. Coupled with data curation filters that actively reject static or settled scenes (§3.2.1), the generative model learned an active steady-state oscillator rather than passive dissipative mechanics.
5. **Anomalous Energy Creation & Limit Cycles**: On low drop heights ($5.3\text{ cm}$), Nano exhibits anomalous energy creation, rebounding to a $30.8\text{ cm}$ apex (**$+580\%$ height increase**, apparent $e_v = 2.41$), or freezing in a proximity-induced levitation attractor (66.7% rate), proving that Nano operates as a kinematic pattern generator with a preferred limit-cycle amplitude and period ($\approx 0.33\text{ s}$) rather than an energy-conserving physical simulator.

- **Methodological Limitations & Confounders**:
  1. *Confounded by Endpoint Mix*: Only 1/12 $S4 - Z4$ and 2/12 $S2 - Z2$ pairs executed on identical queue endpoints. Because diffusers ignores flow shift on Karras schedules and cross-GPU outputs diverge, causal attribution to the vector is unproven; the finding is scoped strictly to "the $S4$ arm has lower measured flow than $Z4$ in this sample" (codex-api-2 #3689).
  2. *Unvalidated Flow Proxy vs Physical Rest*: Neither Farneback optical flow nor qualitative eye codes are planted-validated for physical rest. Flow reduction can reflect disappearance, edge-leaving, or blurring rather than mechanical rest.
  3. *Unclear Codes Cannot Prove Zero Rest*: 0/12 confirmed rest with 9 unclear cases cannot be counted as non-rest or proof of zero physical settling (#3790); the physical outcome remains unconfirmed and unresolved.
- **Causal Mechanism & Theoretical Perspectives (Scout-1 #3761)**:
  - *H-D: Late-Band Motion Damper (Lead Hypothesis)*: Timed prompt swaps in video diffusion models (arXiv:2512.22175) demonstrate that conditioning after $\tau \approx 700\text{--}950/1000$ modulates local pixel rendering and displacements without altering high-level event trajectory plans. In unified 3D attention architectures (like CogVideoX and Cosmos), late denoising steps primarily set local sharpness and spatial variance. When a steering direction acts predominantly after step 10, it suppresses late inter-frame differences (functioning as a smooth continuous velocity damper or "dial") but cannot insert a discrete physical contact/stiction event ("switch"), explaining why contact mechanics and true rest do not materialize (Parmar et al., arXiv:2103.15406; Schäfer et al., arXiv:2607.05966).
  - *H-E: Under-Dosed Early Switch (Alternative)*: If the event plan is determined in the earliest steps ($t \in [0, 10]$), the prompt-contrast signal at step 0 is small relative to seed noise ($\approx 0.2\times$), so an injection gain of $\alpha=4$ may be sub-threshold.
  - *Recipe Implication*: Because internal activation steering produces unconfirmed physical rest with unresolved mechanisms and substantial artifacts, it cannot serve as a reliable drop-in fix candidate for Deliverable 1. User-level prompt conditioning (Rule R) and mid-event release initialization remain the deployable recipe components.
